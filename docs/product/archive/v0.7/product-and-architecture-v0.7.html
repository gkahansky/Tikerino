0. Context and what changed

Guy’s earlier concept (“beat the index” trading levels) is replaced by a stronger one, in his words: “Like Duolingo, but for investing. Learn technical and fundamental analysis through a game. Pattern recognition, value analysis, etc. You learn one small principle, then practice it through a game in a mobile app.”

Why this shape is stronger (already aligned with Guy on WhatsApp, 10 Sep 10:28): 1. The user goal is to learn a skill, not to get lucky over one simulated month. 2. The game scores correct identification and understanding, so it is far less exposed to luck, multiple comparisons, and overfitting than P&L-based play. 3. It is native to mobile: a 2–3 minute lesson + a short exercise batch. 4. TradeSim’s SimEngine stays useful in a new role: generating real point-in-time historical exercises and grading the user’s decision without revealing what happened next.

1. Product positioning

One-liner: A mobile game that teaches you to read markets the way Duolingo teaches languages — one tiny principle at a time, practiced on real historical charts, with instant feedback.

Category: investing-education game (not a broker, not a simulator with real-money hooks, not a signals product).

What makes it defensible: - MVP exercises use synthetic, generator-authored chart sequences, cut point-in-time — the learner never sees the future until after committing to an answer. This avoids redistribution licensing in MVP while preserving the reveal and audit discipline; licensed real-ticker data is a post-MVP path (§6–7). - A grading engine with audit identity (every exercise carries dataSnapshotId + generator version) inherited from TradeSim’s locked architecture principles. - A pedagogical spine (structured curriculum + spaced review), not just a drill tool.

Competitive landscape (indicative web check, 10 Sep 2026 — not exhaustive): - CandleDojo — “the chart-reading gym”, markets itself as “Duolingo for chart reading” — real historical charts, long/short calls. https://candledojo.app/ - ChartDojo — replay real market data, AI feedback. https://chartdojo.io/ (also an open-source “Chart Dojo” XP/streak trainer: https://github.com/michaelsboost/ChartDojo) - CHART QUEST (CRAZYSTOCK, Japan) — RPG-style chart-learning game; appears delisted as of Aug 2026. https://applion.jp/チャートクエスト/android-com.crazystock.game.chartquest/ - ChartLingo — step-by-step chart learning for adults + kids’ “chart academy”. https://www.chartlingo.ai/ - TradePal — “Learn Investing” gamified lessons. https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.zay.traderpal - TradingPal — AI trading coach. https://tradingpalapp.com/ - patternIQ — interactive trading / financial literacy. https://apps.apple.com/us/app/patterniq/id6751753909 - Stack — bite-sized lessons + $10k virtual portfolio. https://www.stackinvest.app/ - Learn by MyWallSt — text-lesson investing course. https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=rubicoin.rubicoinlearn - Up With Stocks — gamified market lessons. https://upwithstocks.com/ - ChartGame / Stock Chart Trading Game — chart-replay trading practice. https://apps.apple.com/us/app/chartgame-trading-simulator/id6761395602

3. Core game loop (mobile)

One loop = 2–4 minutes. Already presented to Guy and not objected to:

  1. Learn — one small principle as a single card (≤60 words + one annotated mini-chart). Example: “support and resistance”.
  2. Guided example — the principle applied to one real historical chart, step by step.
  3. Practice — 5–10 real historical charts cut before the outcome. The learner marks the pattern, drags a level, or picks an action, and (optionally) says how confident they are.
  4. Instant feedback — what was identified correctly, what was missed, then the reveal: the chart animates forward to show what actually happened.
  5. Progress — XP, streak, lesson crown level; tough exercises auto-bookmark into the spaced-review queue.
  6. Market exam — every few lessons, a no-hints mixed set on symbols/dates never seen in training.

Design rules for the loop: one idea per screen; the chart is the hero; feedback ≤40 words plus the visual reveal; no dashboards or tables on a phone screen (lesson learned from TradeSim Stitch v1, which Guy rejected as too dense).

4. MVP curriculum — two locked topics

Scope lock (Guy, 11 Sep 15:53 IDT): MVP content covers exactly two topic families:

  1. Topic 1 — Trading fundamentals (מושגי יסוד במסחר): what a security/ticker/chart is; price and percentage change; bid/ask and spread at an educational level; market versus limit order concepts; long versus short as vocabulary only; volume; timeframe; and the difference between historical observation and a recommendation.
  2. Topic 2 — Candlestick reading (קריאת נרות): OHLC, body, upper/lower wick, bullish/bearish candle conventions, candle range and body size, single-candle interpretation in context, and short sequences that teach what can and cannot be inferred.

Each topic uses the core loop: short principle card → guided example → synthetic chart exercises → immediate explanation and forward reveal → mixed exam. The content pack may contain multiple levels inside these two topics, but no third topic enters MVP.

Out of MVP: support/resistance, named multi-candle patterns, indicators, fundamental/company analysis, options, crypto, broker connection, live trading, real-money mechanics, real-ticker datasets, and A/B content variants. These may enter only after friends/family feedback and a scope decision (§11).

5. Progression, scoring, feedback

Progression: Duolingo-style path map; each lesson has crown levels 0–5 (mastery via spaced review, not one-shot completion). Daily streak. Weekly market exam.

Scoring (anti-luck by design): - Base 10 XP per correct identification; difficulty multiplier ×1–2 by exercise clarity tier. - Hint costs 50% of the exercise’s XP. - Small speed bonus, hard-capped — speed is never worth more than accuracy. - ~~Calibration score~~ DEFERRED from MVP (Critic Phase C: Reject/defer — added complexity, low marginal teaching value at MVP scale). Post-MVP backlog: optional self-rated confidence vs outcome, Brier-style. Teaches honest self-assessment — the meta-skill of trading. - We score identification quality, not trade P&L. No leaderboard of “returns” in MVP.

Feedback grammar (every exercise): 1. What you saw (correct marks). 2. What you missed (unmarked pattern elements). 3. What happened next (animated forward reveal). All copy ≤40 words, pattern name repeated, one “why” sentence.

Retention mechanics allowed in MVP: streaks, crowns, spaced review, exam unlocks. Not allowed: loot boxes, pay-to-skip, loss-streak punishment, real-money hooks of any kind.

6. Exercise generation — point-in-time, no look-ahead

The integrity core. MVP uses synthetic generated charts while inheriting TradeSim’s locked principles: point-in-time reveal discipline, deterministic generation, and full audit identity.

  • MVP exercise = (syntheticSeriesId, window_end T). Learner sees generated candles through T only. The generator creates the complete sequence, but the client receives post-T candles only after answer lock; reveal covers the configured synthetic horizon.
  • Server-side window cutting: the client bundle never contains post-T data; the reveal is fetched only after the answer is recorded. No look-ahead by construction, not by convention.
  • Generation and sampling: the reused SimEngine produces deterministic OHLCV-like sequences from a versioned scenario specification and seeded RNG. Scenario families cover trend/range, volatility, liquidity/spread examples, candle anatomy, noise, and ambiguous cases. Seeds and parameters are persisted so every exercise replays bit-for-bit.
  • No implied market provenance: synthetic charts are clearly labeled educational/generated data. They must not carry a real ticker, company, date, or claim to reproduce an actual episode. Plausibility QA checks OHLC consistency, gaps, volatility, volume shape, and lesson-target visibility.
  • Content QA pipeline: automated invariant and clarity checks → human review of a sampled render set. Each exercise carries exerciseId + syntheticSeriesId + scenarioSpecVersion + seed + generatorVersion + curriculumVersion. The reserved dataSnapshotId field remains nullable in MVP and becomes mandatory only for licensed real-data exercises later.
  • Real-data compatibility: the exercise interface retains a future source discriminator (synthetic | licensed-market-data) and the dataSnapshotId audit slot. Split/dividend adjustment policy applies only when licensed real data is introduced.
  • Presentation: MVP charts show no ticker or date because they are synthetic. When real data is later licensed, anonymized ticker/date presentation remains the default candidate until learning, disclosure, and license requirements are reviewed.
  • Offline rule — deferred reveal: a graded item may be answered offline, but the answer locks locally and the reveal waits for reconnect and server acknowledgement. The client never caches post-T bars. Store capturedOffline and assignmentSnapshotAt with the answer; retries are idempotent.
  • Integrity detection: CI runs a reveal-before-lock negative test and scans every exercise payload for any bar after T. A production replay canary continuously regenerates a fixed seed set and compares content/grading hashes; any mismatch blocks release or triggers the §18 kill path.

7. Content & data architecture

  • Lessons: versioned JSON content packs (principle cards, guided examples, exercise manifests). Downloadable for offline play; curriculumVersion pinned per user until they opt into an update.
  • Future localization model (deferred; MVP is English only): language-neutral records own conceptId, answer schema, grading rule, chart/exercise references, and difficulty metadata. Localized content is keyed by BCP 47 locale and stable content IDs: question[locale][promptVariantId], explanation[locale], hint[locale], glossary terms, and accessibility labels. When localization is activated, a release manifest pins every localized asset and rejects publication when a required key is missing; there is no silent fallback inside a graded exercise.
  • MVP chart source: deterministic synthetic generation only. Scenario specs, seeds, generated series, and render manifests are versioned and stored as immutable exercise inputs. No vendor market-data feed is required for MVP.
  • Licensing decision path (locked for MVP): synthetic charts mean zero third-party market-data redistribution and no data-license dependency for MVP. Real-ticker exercises are deferred. Before any real vendor data is ingested or shown to friends/family, research each candidate vendor’s exact display, derived-data, storage, and redistribution terms and price the required tier; only a vendor/license explicitly covering the intended product use may pass the gate.
  • Storage: Postgres (users, progress, content manifests, grading records) + object storage (chart window payloads, rendered assets).

8. App & backend architecture

  • Client (locked direction): ship MVP as a mobile-first React web PWA in TypeScript. Use responsive, touch-first candlestick components and installable/offline-safe lesson shells. The final product direction is React Native. Keep exercise generation, grading, progression/state machines, localization keys, analytics events, audit identity, and domain types in framework-neutral TypeScript packages shared by both clients; only the screen/navigation/platform-adapter layer is rewritten for React Native.
  • Engine role: reuse and narrow TradeSim’s SimEngine as a deterministic synthetic scenario generator, not as a backtest or live-market simulator. It owns seeded chart generation, scenario invariants, future-reveal payloads, difficulty parameters, and replay metadata. Grading remains concept-specific and deterministic outside the generator.
  • MVP locale: English only. Hebrew and all i18n delivery are deferred.
  • Hebrew-readiness checklist (future phase, not MVP): keep user-facing copy behind stable keys; choose fonts with complete Hebrew glyphs and compatible metrics; use semantic start/end layout primitives; define bidi isolation for mixed Hebrew, Latin, symbols, and numbers; maintain a bilingual trading glossary; mirror navigation, cards, directional icons, gestures, and transitions while preserving chronological chart-axis direction; require RTL screenshots, screen-reader checks, and fluent editorial review before enabling he-IL.
  • Backend (Node/TypeScript):
  • content-service — lesson packs, versioning.
  • exercise-service — sampler + PIT window cutter (server-side reveal gating).
  • grading-service — deterministic per-pattern grading rules with tolerances; no LLM in the grading hot path (LLM only in content QA and explanations).
  • progress-service — XP, crowns, streaks, spaced-review scheduler.
  • analytics — event sink (see §10).
  • Determinism & audit: every graded answer stores exerciseId + source identity (syntheticSeriesId or dataSnapshotId) + generatorVersion + curriculumVersion + answer payload + capturedOffline + assignmentSnapshotAt. Replays are bit-for-bit reproducible (TradeSim engine-audit standard).
  • Infra posture: PWA hosting + managed API/storage, feature flags, and server-driven content toggles. No vendor market feed, real ticker, or real-time market data anywhere in MVP. Preserve a clean boundary so licensed immutable snapshots can be added later without changing grading contracts.

9. Safety & education boundaries

  • Education, not advice. Persistent framing: patterns are historical tendencies, not profit promises. No “buy/sell now” language, ever.
  • No real money: no broker linking, no live trading, no deposits, no paper-portfolio with real-time prices in MVP.
  • Disclaimers: past performance / simulation notice on every exam screen and on the reveal screen, not just at onboarding.
  • Audience protection: 18+ gate assumed pending the audience decision; if a kids/teens track ever appears (ChartLingo plays there), it needs a separate design and legal pass.
  • Honest difficulty: ambiguous windows stay in the curriculum labeled as ambiguous — the app teaches that sometimes the right answer is “unclear”, which most competitors avoid teaching.
  • Privacy and erasure: analytics/audit records use pseudonymous subjectId, never email as the join key. Keep the identity-to-subject mapping separately access-controlled. Publish purpose, consent basis, and retention windows; on GDPR/Israel PPL erasure, delete identity/profile/content where required and either delete or irreversibly de-identify retained aggregate/audit records. “Immutable” means tamper-evident while lawfully retained, not exempt from erasure law.

10. Analytics

  • Events: lesson_started/completed, exercise_answered (pattern family, correct, time-to-answer, hints, confidence), reveal_viewed, exam_completed (score + calibration), streak_maintained/broken, review_queue_served.
  • North-star: Week-1 retention × exam-accuracy improvement over a learner’s first 10 exams.
  • Guardrail metrics: hint dependency rate, rage-quit points (drop-off by exercise), ambiguous-exercise complaint rate.
  • All analytics are product analytics; no trading behavior data is sold or shared.

Learner model and mastery service

  • A mastery-service owns a versioned skill DAG: trading vocabulary → candle anatomy → contextual interpretation. Items declare the skills they teach/test; prerequisites gate progression.
  • MVP starts with rubric/heuristic item calibration, then moves to a 1PL/Rasch-style model when response volume supports stable difficulty estimates. Adaptive serving targets a 70–85% success band so practice is effortful without becoming random failure.
  • Spaced review uses a named algorithm: FSRS, with parameters versioned and tuned only after enough retention observations. Mastery estimates and scheduling versions remain replayable.
  • Explicit content cost: plan roughly 400–800 calibrated items per locale for a mature two-topic bank, plus the sealed exam bank. Every added locale multiplies authoring, review, accessibility, QA, and calibration work; this is one reason Hebrew is deferred.

11. Locked delivery path

  1. MVP build: mobile-first React web PWA; English only; the two locked topics in §4; synthetic generated charts; one default palette, one logo, one English wording per item, and a fixed introductory difficulty path. Experiment plumbing exists but is dormant (§18).
  2. Friends/family feedback — explicitly not an experiment: everyone receives the same build. Collect first impressions of UX and find obvious bugs only. No assignment, variants, holdout, experiment metrics, or promotion gate applies in this phase.
  3. Post-MVP expansion: activate selected variants, begin controlled learning experiments, add marketing work, and add more levels/topics based on evidence. Real-ticker exercises remain behind the per-vendor redistribution-license gate.
  4. Final client after validation: build the production screen/navigation layer in React Native after PWA validation. Reuse the framework-neutral TypeScript engine, logic, state, content, localization, grading, analytics, and audit packages; rewrite only the screen and platform-adapter layer.

12. Visual & design system

Direction from Guy: clean, simple to understand, young and fun — not heavy or dense.

Principles 1. One idea per screen. If a screen needs a scrollbar on a phone, it is two screens. 2. The chart is the hero: full-bleed, generous whitespace, zero chrome competing with it. 3. Big tap targets (≥48px), thumb-zone actions, max 3 text styles per screen. 4. Celebrate: spring-motion reveals (150–250ms), haptic tick on correct, confetti only on exam passes. 5. Friendly voice: short sentences, zero jargon before it is taught.

Tokens - Token namespaces: brand.* controls navigation, CTAs, rewards, surfaces, and identity; data.* controls chart meaning. Palette changes may change brand.* only. - Frozen data colors (Guy, 11 Sep 16:14 IDT): bullish candles are always green and bearish candles always red, independent of brand palette. Freeze data.candle.up / data.candle.down and pair color with non-color encoding: hollow/filled bodies, direction glyph/label, and screen-reader text. Never retint data encoding in a palette experiment. - Shared brand colors: ink navy #0F2B46, coral #FF6B57, sunshine #FFB020, paper #FAFAF7. Dark mode deferred. - Locked future brand-palette pair: palette.original-green (#10B981 primary) and palette.violet (#7C3AED primary). 17. Duolingo look-and-feel exposure — orientation (11 Sep 2026)

Guy asked (11 Sep 11:47 IDT): the Stitch screens feel very Duolingo — is there lawsuit risk? Orientation for a founder decision, not legal advice. Formal IP counsel (US + Israel) is required before launch and before any name lock (§13).

What Duolingo protects, and how actively: - Documented, deliberate identity: Feather Green (#58CC02), Duo the owl, Feather Bold type, illustrated character system — published brand guidelines (https://design.duolingo.com/identity). - Registered marks, incl. the owl logo (USPTO serial 88703483: https://www.trademarkelite.com/trademark/trademark-detail/88703483/logo). - Active trademark enforcement: Duolingo, Inc. (Latham & Watkins) opposed the mark “UGO LINGO” at the USPTO TTAB — opposition 91304428 (https://ttabvue.uspto.gov/ttabvue/v?pno=91304428&pty=OPP); the opposed application was abandoned after the inter-partes decision. Reading: Duolingo polices “-LINGO” names. The current working name Tikerino avoids the suffix. - Copyright enforcement exists but targets piracy: DMCA takedown of a cracked Duolingo app repo (GitHub, Apr 2025: https://github.com/github/dmca/blob/master/2025/04/2025-04-23-duolingo.md). No public record found of Duolingo suing a look-alike learning app.

Mechanics are not ownable. - Trade dress protects a product’s total look only when distinctive, non-functional, and confusingly similar. 18. Experiment architecture — dormant registry and post-MVP controls

Current-stage scope. MVP and friends/family use no variants. Implement only the deterministic assignment contract, immutable variant registry, persisted audit fields, and kill-test hooks now. Activate experiments only after the UX/bug phase and a passing power calculation.

Experiment classes

  • Brand: brand.* palette/theme only. Logo is excluded and picked once. data.* chart colors are immutable.
  • Pedagogy: question phrasing or difficulty. At most one live pedagogy experiment at a time. Within-subject phrasing randomization remains an open design question; do not implement it without Guy’s ruling.
  • Integrity: controlled validation of delivery/audit mechanisms, never relaxation of PIT, grading, privacy, safety, or data-license rules.

Canonical deterministic assignment

  • Create a random stable installId on first launch; use it as subjectId while anonymous. On signup, atomically merge install history into the account’s canonical pseudonymous subject and persist an alias/merge record so a learner is not double-counted or reassigned.
  • Each immutable ExperimentDefinition stores experimentId, class, eligibility, variants, integer allocation ranges, frozen per-experiment salt, assignmentSecretVersion, metrics, power plan, guardrails, and lifecycle. A material change creates a new ID.
  • Canonical input uses length-prefixed UTF-8 fields in this exact order: experimentId | subjectId | assignmentContext | frozenSalt. Compute HMAC-SHA256(secret[assignmentSecretVersion], canonicalBytes); interpret the first 64 unsigned bits as big-endian and reduce modulo 10,000. Variant weights are integer basis points summing to the experiment’s eligible range; variants map to contiguous half-open ranges in registry order.
  • Persist an AssignmentRecord before exposure: subject, experiment/variant, bucket, eligibility snapshot, assignment context, salt, secret version, algorithm version, assignedAt, and assignmentSnapshotAt. Assignment is sticky and replayable.
  • Ramps use a monotonic eligible bucket range (for example [0,500) → [0,2500)); existing subjects never leave or change variants when traffic grows. Keep a permanent 5% global holdout excluded from all product experiments.

Audit identity, offline, and privacy

  • Exercise/answer identity: exerciseId + source identity + generatorVersion + curriculumVersion + subjectId + experimentId/variantId + assignmentAlgorithmVersion + assignmentSecretVersion + locale + localizationVersion + capturedOffline + assignmentSnapshotAt + answer payload/hash. Dormant MVP experiment fields are null/none.
  • Offline answers lock until reconnect; reveal occurs only after server acknowledgement. The saved assignment snapshot travels with the answer. Replay resolves exact registry/content/generator versions.
  • Subject IDs are pseudonymous; identity mapping, retention, consent, and erasure follow §9. Audit immutability does not override GDPR or Israel PPL rights.

Learning measurement

  • No experiment is defined or launched without a passing power/MDE calculation and preregistered primary outcome, harm boundary, exposure window, and slice plan. Use group-sequential boundaries or always-valid inference for planned monitoring; do not repeatedly peek at ordinary fixed-horizon p-values.
  • Difficulty tests use a common, treatment-invariant, frozen, sealed holdout exam bank: separate seed, no reveal, no hints, no XP, inaccessible to training selection, and chance-corrected scoring. Refreshing the bank creates a new version and analysis boundary.
  • Primary outcomes are holdout identification quality, transfer, and delayed retention. Engagement cannot win while learning crosses the harm boundary. Monitor sample-ratio mismatch (SRM) continuously; confirmed SRM automatically freezes ramp and new assignments.

Rollout and kill detection

  • Post-MVP rollout may progress 5% → 25% → 50% → 100% only after power, accessibility, replay, allocation, and guardrail checks. The 5% global holdout remains untouched.
  • Automated kill triggers: SRM; CI/canary reveal-before-lock failure; payload scan finds any bar after T; replay canary hash mismatch; incomplete audit identity; wrong grading; privacy/license/safety breach; or accessibility blocker. Freeze new assignments, preserve incident evidence lawfully, revert to safe control, and open an incident.
  • Statistical rollback: apply the preregistered group-sequential/always-valid rule. Roll back when the confidence sequence crosses the harm boundary for learning or a declared high-risk slice, regardless of engagement.

Implementation boundary. Keep the experiment registry/assignment module separate behind stable interfaces; whether the backend ships as a modular monolith or separate service remains an open architecture decision. Feature flags are emergency delivery controls, not experiment definitions.
